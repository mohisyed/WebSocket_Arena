TICKET 05 — CONTROL FRAMES: CLOSE HANDSHAKE + PING/PONG KEEPALIVE
================================================================================
PHASE: MVP
DEPENDS ON: 04

GOAL
  Handle the three control opcodes and detect dead connections.

WHAT TO DO
  - Close (0x8): on receiving one, send a close frame back, then close the TCP
    socket. The close handshake is in-band and bidirectional.
  - Ping (0x9): reply with Pong (0xA) echoing the ping's payload.
  - Pong (0xA): treat as a liveness signal.
  - Optionally send your own periodic pings; track a per-connection "last seen"
    timestamp and reap zombie connections.

CONCEPTS THIS TEACHES
  - Control vs data frames
  - In-band close handshake vs a raw TCP FIN
  - Keepalive/heartbeat design; liveness/timeout detection; graceful teardown

GOTCHAS
  - Control frames must be <=125 bytes payload and never fragmented.
  - A close frame may carry a 2-byte status code + UTF-8 reason.
  - ALWAYS remove the connection from your registry on close/error or you'll
    broadcast to dead sockets and leak.
  - Browsers send a close frame on tab close — handle it without spewing
    exceptions.

DEFINITION OF DONE
  - Closing the browser tab -> clean server-side disconnect log (no stack trace).
  - A ping is answered by a pong.
  - A client you kill (pull network) is reaped within your timeout window.

RESOURCES
  - RFC 6455 §5.5 (Control Frames), §5.5.1 (Close), §5.5.2/§5.5.3 (Ping/Pong),
    §7 (Closing the Connection)
    https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6455

DAY-JOB TRANSFER
  Ping/pong is the echo-test/heartbeat keepalive you use to keep an acquirer or
  Tandem link alive and to detect a half-open socket.
